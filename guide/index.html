French Quarter

Bar

Beach On Bourbon

4.4★ · 3,117 Google reviews

Two blocks off Canal, The Beach is the room Bourbon Street built for a crowd. A stage that runs live bands and DJs into the small hours, a dance floor that fills by ten, a wall of screens for game day, and a courtyard out back with its own bar and a fountain, which is where you go when you need five minutes and a breeze.

Order: The Hurricane, Live Music, The Courtyard

Local tip: Book VIP seating by text for a big group, then send the half that needs a breather to the courtyard. Everyone stays in the same building.

Hours, menu & photos · Map · 227 Bourbon St

Irish pub

Ryan's Irish Pub Inc

4.5★ · 875 Google reviews

Ryan's sits on the corner of Decatur and Iberville, three blocks from Jackson Square and a world away from the daiquiri machines. Sixteen beers on tap, a wall of bottles, a full bar, and a proper pour of Guinness. The floor is old tile, the bar is old wood, and the bathrooms are, reviewers keep noting with some surprise, clean.

Order: Sixteen Taps, The Guinness Pour, Irish Car Bombs

Local tip: Grab a muffuletta from Central Grocery two blocks down, then come here for the pint. That is the local move.

Hours, menu & photos · Map · 241 Decatur St

Grocery store

Matassa's Market

4.4★ · 425 Google reviews

Matassa's has been the neighborhood grocery at Dauphine and St. Philip for a century. Fresh fruit out front, a liquor and wine selection that surprises people, a deli counter that has fed the Quarter's bartenders and residents through every era. The recent renovation made it look new without making it feel new.

Order: Shawarma, Kofta Burger, The Deli

Local tip: Grab a shawarma and a bottle from the wall and walk two blocks to the river. The Quarter's cheapest great dinner.

Hours, menu & photos · Map · 1001 Dauphine St

Bar

Boondock Saint

4.7★ · 379 Google reviews

Named for the cult movie and run like a neighborhood pub, Boondock Saint is the French Quarter bar locals keep for themselves. It is small. It is dark in the good way. The Guinness pours right, the jukebox has taste, and the bartenders, Lacey among them, are the reason half the reviews exist.

Order: The Guinness, The Jukebox, The Bartenders

Local tip: Weekday afternoon is the locals' hour. Weekend late is the party. Both are worth it for different reasons.

Hours, menu & photos · Map · 731 St Peter

Bar

The Upper Quarter

4.7★ · 358 Google reviews

The Upper Quarter calls itself the best neighborhood bar in the French Quarter, and the reviews are not arguing. It is small and mighty: a corner room at Bienville and Burgundy, bartenders named in nearly every review, a crowd that talks to strangers, and pudding shots for reasons that make sense after the second one.

Order: Crawfish Boils, Pudding Shots, The Bartenders

Local tip: Sit at the corner of the bar and say hello. That's the entire strategy. You'll have five friends by the second drink.

Hours, menu & photos · Map · 1000 Bienville St

Central Business District

Pub

Cajun Mikes Pub & Grub

4.3★ · 1,091 Google reviews

Cajun Mike's has been on Baronne Street since 2000, which in this part of the CBD makes it an elder. It is a bar first: sixteen-ish stools, a long wood top, TVs for the game, and bartenders who refill before you ask. Regulars call it a hole in the wall and mean it as the highest compliment.

Order: Hot Roast Beef Po'boy, Cochon de Lait, Boudin Balls

Local tip: Two blocks from the Roosevelt and the Saenger. Perfect pre-show, and it is where the bartenders from the fancy places drink after.

Hours, menu & photos · Map · 116 Baronne St

Vietnamese restaurant

Viet Orleans Bistro

4.3★ · 852 Google reviews

Viet Orleans opened on the corner of Baronne and Gravier to feed the office towers and hotels around it, and it still does that better than anywhere in the CBD: a bowl of pho lands in about six minutes at the lunch rush. The broth is clear and long-simmered, the herbs come piled high, and the brisket is the cut regulars ask for.

Order: Seafood Pho, Volcano Roll, Grilled Pork Vermicelli

Local tip: Happy hour is the play: half the sushi menu, cold sake, and the room to yourself before the hotel crowd finds it.

Hours, menu & photos · Map · 300 Baronne St

Cigar shop

Don Leoncio Cigars Bar

4.4★ · 602 Google reviews

Don Leoncio is the lounge you hope exists when you walk off Bourbon Street wanting somewhere quieter. The neon says cigar bar, the room says gentleman's club without the pretension: deep leather couches, a long bar of whiskeys and rums, a humidor room glowing in the back, and a game on the screen.

Order: Hand-Rolled Cigars, Cigar & Whiskey Pairing, The Couches

Local tip: Canal St side, a block from the Marriott and the Sheraton. Best hour is late, after dinner, when the lounge settles down.

Hours, menu & photos · Map · 430 Canal St

Bar

Little Bar on Gravier

4.8★ · 415 Google reviews

Half a block from the big hotels on Gravier, a door you'd walk past. Inside: a short bar, a jukebox, a bartender who will know your name by the second drink, and pours that reviewers describe as heavy with a straight face. It is the bar the convention crowd finds by accident and comes back to on purpose.

Order: The Bloody Mary, The Sazerac, Heavy Pours

Local tip: Ask the bartender where to eat. They send people to the right places and take it personally if you come back unhappy.

Hours, menu & photos · Map · 510 Gravier St

Mid-City

Grocery store

Banks Meat Market

4.1★ · 731 Google reviews

Banks Meat Market anchors the corner of Broad and Banks, and everyone within ten blocks has a Banks order. The butcher case in back runs deep: fresh cuts, smoked sausage by the pound, hot sausage patties, turkey necks, whatever the week's plate needs. The hot counter up front turns it into lunch.

Order: Hot Roast Beef Po'boy, The 32-Inch, Hot Sausage by the Pound

Local tip: Late night, the hot counter is still going when everything else on Broad has closed. Roast beef at eleven is a Mid-City rite.

Hours, menu & photos · Map · 325 S Broad St

Bakery

Norma's Sweets Bakery

4.3★ · 531 Google reviews

Norma's Sweets started as a Latin bakery and grew into the neighborhood's kitchen. The case up front holds tres leches, empanadas, guava-and-cheese pastries, and pan dulce. The grill in back turns out Cuban sandwiches, baleadas, tamales, ribs, and a plate of the day. The shelves in between are a real Latin grocery: Nicaraguan coffee, produce, meat.

Order: Tres Leches, Cuban Sandwich, Baleadas

Local tip: Order the Cuban and a horchata, then walk the aisles while it presses. You'll leave with coffee and pastries you didn't plan on.

Hours, menu & photos · Map · 2925 Bienville St

Bar

Mick's Irish Pub

4.5★ · 375 Google reviews

Mick's Irish Pub sits on the corner of Bienville and Bernadotte with a full game room: pool table, dart boards, pinball, ping pong, shuffleboard, video games. It is quiet on weekdays and lively on weekends and always fairly priced. The bartenders will make whatever you want as long as the bottle exists.

Seventh Ward

Donut shop

Tastee Donuts & Deli

4.2★ · 459 Google reviews

Tastee Donuts was once a citywide chain, and this Esplanade Avenue shop carries the name and the recipes into the present. The buttermilk drops are the reason people cross town. The apple fritters are the size of a plate. Cake donuts, glazed, jelly, cinnamon rolls, all fried in small batches.

Order: Buttermilk Drops, Apple Fritter, Grits & Eggs Breakfast

Local tip: Ask for whatever just came out. Buttermilk drops sell out first; grits and eggs are the sleeper breakfast.

Hours, menu & photos · Map · 2549 Esplanade Ave

Seafood restaurant

Danny's Seafood

4.2★ · 403 Google reviews

Danny's sits on a side street in the 7th Ward and feeds the neighborhood out of a counter window. Boiled shrimp and snow crab most days, crawfish when it's running, pig feet and turkey necks slow-cooked in the same spicy boil. The heat is real; ask for mild if you're not from here.

Order: Spicy Turkey Necks, Boiled Shrimp, Snow Crab

Local tip: Friday afternoon is the rush. Call your order in, pick it up hot, and get the potatoes.

Hours, menu & photos · Map · 1812 Onzaga St

Bar

Seal's Class Act

4.4★ · 399 Google reviews

Seal's is the 7th Ward's living room after dark. Ms. Seal has run it long enough that reviewers call her family. Live bands most weekends, a DJ between sets, a dance floor, bar and table seating, and a small cover at the door that keeps the room grown.

Order: The Live Band, Ms. Seal, The Food Trucks

Local tip: Get there before the band starts for a table. Cover at the door, one-drink minimum, then the night is yours.

Hours, menu & photos · Map · 2169 Aubry St

Bywater

Bar

Markey's Bar

4.5★ · 486 Google reviews

Markey's sits on the corner of Louisa and Royal in the Bywater, in a building that has been pouring drinks since long before the neighborhood got interesting. The black-and-white photo behind the bar of the old crew under the 7-Up sign tells you how far back it goes. The locals inside tell you it never really changed.

Order: Crawfish Boil, The Guinness, Shuffleboard & Darts

Local tip: Bike over on the Crescent Park path and lock up out front. Kickoff Sundays fill by noon; get there for the pregame.

Hours, menu & photos · Map · 640 Louisa St

Lounge bar

BJ's Lounge

4.6★ · 388 Google reviews

BJ's Lounge has been the Bywater's corner bar since before the Bywater was a destination. Cash only, cheap drinks, a jukebox, a courtyard out back, and Monday nights that belong to the blues. Little Freddie King, well into his eighties, still plays here. Reviewers say don't miss it. They are right.

Order: Little Freddie King, The Courtyard, The Jukebox

Local tip: Walk over from Crescent Park on a Monday night. If Freddie's playing, get there early and stand near the front.

Hours, menu & photos · Map · 4301 Burgundy St

Uptown/Carrollton

Donut shop

City Donuts & Café

4★ · 1,034 Google reviews

Every neighborhood needs one place that is open when nothing else is. On South Claiborne it is City Donuts. Glazed, powdered, cake, blueberry, twists, and the beignets that reviewers drive across town for, fried in small batches all night long. The drive-thru is the reason half the city knows the place without ever having parked.

Order: Glazed Beignets, Shrimp & Grits, The Donut Case

Local tip: Ask what just came out of the fryer. Whatever it is, get a dozen of that and let them box the rest.

Bayou St. John

Bar

Pal's Lounge

4.6★ · 707 Google reviews

Pal's has been the bar on the corner of Rendon and St. Philip long enough that people give directions by it. It is a dive in the way New Orleans means the word: good lighting, a jukebox somebody curated, bartenders who move fast and remember your drink, dogs on the patio, and prices that let you stay for the second round.

Order: The Cocktail List, Local Beer, Pop-Up Nights

Local tip: Tuesday is pop-up night and the patio fills by eight. Walk over from the Bayou St. John end and you'll never fight for parking.

Faubourg Lafayette

Brunch restaurant

Cafe Porche & Snowbar

4.9★ · 644 Google reviews

Cafe Porche sits in a little white house on Baronne Street with tables out front under the trees. Mrs. Porche runs the room. The reviews say the same thing over and over: the food was amazing, and the people were kinder than they had to be. In a city with a lot of brunch, that combination is rarer than it sounds.

Order: Praline French Toast, Shrimp & Grits, Fried Catfish & Eggs

Local tip: Come before 10:30 on Saturday or plan to wait on the porch, which honestly is not a bad way to spend twenty minutes.

Hours, menu & photos · Map · 1625 Baronne St

Gentilly Terrace

Dessert shop

New Orleans Snowball

4.5★ · 627 Google reviews

Ask anyone in Gentilly where they get a snowball and you'll hear this corner. The ice is shaved fluffy, not crunchy, the syrup list runs long enough that regulars have a standing order, and the condensed-milk drizzle is the move the reviews keep mentioning. Cheesecake-stuffed is the order for the people who know.

Order: Double Cherry, Condensed Milk, Stuffed Snowball, Nachos & Po'boys

Local tip: Bring cash. The line moves fastest that way, and the small is plenty.

Hours, menu & photos · Map · 4313 Elysian Fields Ave

Gert Town

Chinese restaurant

Chinese Kitchen

4.1★ · 1,799 Google reviews

Chinese Kitchen is a plain takeout spot on S Carrollton, with a red sign out front and a parking lot you can pull into. Ask anyone who has ordered here and they'll bring up the egg rolls first. Reviewers mention them in 175 reviews, more than any other dish. Reviewers also name the shrimp fried rice and boneless chicken. The food comes in foam boxes, and a lot of it was homemade, one visitor from New York said.

Order: Egg rolls, Shrimp fried rice, Boneless chicken

Local tip: Bring cash, phone ahead if you're in a hurry, and eat the fried food while it's hot. Two reviewers said the rice came out hard.

Hours, menu & photos · Map · 3327 S Carrollton Ave

Marigny

Bar

The John

4.3★ · 571 Google reviews

Two blocks off Frenchmen and a world away from it, The John is where the Marigny drinks when it doesn't feel like being a tourist. The toilet-themed decor is a running joke that became an identity. The drinks are cheap and heavy-handed. The jukebox is good. There is a ping-pong table and a pool table and, reviewers keep noting, a genuinely clean bathroom.

Order: The Well Drink, Ping Pong, The Jukebox

Local tip: Late on a weeknight is the sweet spot. The Frenchmen crowd thins out and the ping-pong table opens up.

Hours, menu & photos · Map · 2040 Burgundy St

New Orleans East Area

Seafood restaurant

Lucky Jean Seafood

4.2★ · 805 Google reviews

Lucky Jean's is the kind of place you find by asking someone from the East where they get their seafood. The answer comes fast. The little building on Chef Menteur Highway with the shrimp boat on the sign has been frying, boiling, and stuffing po'boys for the neighborhood for decades, and the regulars who have been coming since before the storm are still coming.

Order: Fried Shrimp, Shrimp Po'boy, Boiled Crawfish

Local tip: Call your order in. The lunch line is the neighborhood's, and it moves faster than it looks.

South 7th Ward

Bar

Bertha's Place

4.4★ · 673 Google reviews

Bertha's Place is the kind of neighborhood club New Orleans used to be full of and now protects. St. Bernard Avenue, a corner bar with a stage, a dance floor that fills after eleven, and a crowd that dresses for it. Grown and sexy, as one reviewer put it. Birthday parties book the back.

Order: The Live Band, Pork Chop Plate, The Dance Floor

Local tip: Go on a live-band night and get there before ten to get a table. The band starts around eleven and the floor is gone by midnight.

St. Claude

Chicken wings restaurant

Who Dat Wings & More

4.2★ · 373 Google reviews

Who Dat Wings & More is a counter on Franklin Avenue that does one thing seriously. Wings fried crisp, tossed to order in a long sauce list, and the fries that people plan around: cajun ranch, garlic parmesan, loaded with everything. Corn nuggets on the side because it's New Orleans.

Order: Sweet Chili Wings, Loaded Fries, Honey BBQ

Local tip: Order ahead by phone on game days. Sweet chili and garlic parm, half and half, is the local move.

West End

Bakery

Chez Pierre Bakery Lakeview

4.3★ · 63 Google reviews

Chez Pierre is the Lakeview bakery that does two things at once and does both properly. The front case is French: croissants, eclairs, fruit tarts, Eiffel Tower chocolates, king cake in season, and layer cakes decorated to order for birthdays, weddings, and Saints Sundays. The frozen matcha has a following.

Order: Breakfast Croissant, Banh Mi, Custom Cakes

Local tip: Order the cake by phone, then pick up a banh mi and a frozen matcha when you come to get it. Nobody leaves with just the cake.
